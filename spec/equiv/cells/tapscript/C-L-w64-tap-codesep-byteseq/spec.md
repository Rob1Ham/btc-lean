# Cell `tapscript/C-L-w64-tap-codesep-byteseq`

SCR-167 is the `m_codeseparator_pos` rule: it starts at `0xFFFFFFFF`, an
executed `OP_CODESEPARATOR` records the **0-based parsed-opcode index**
(`opcode_pos`), and the legacy `pbegincodehash` byte offset is a separate
field.  This cell models both fields over a bounded, byte-level script:
six symbolic bytes, every `GetScriptOp` form, a symbolic per-parsed-opcode
`fExec` mask, and an observable that exposes `codeSep`, `codeSepPos`,
`opcodeIndex`, a parse-failure bit, the byte cursor, and a separation bit.

The wave32 cell `tapscript/C-L-w32-tap-codesep-pos` proved the counter over
a *pre-decoded* stream and explicitly left `pbegincodehash` out of scope.
This cell keeps the parse as a modelled step (so push payload length really
does move the byte cursor) and adds the legacy offset and the separation
invariant.

## What the two lanes prove

- **Alive2** (`--bidirectional`, symbolic): the C source slice and the Lean
  transcription are equivalent for every six-byte script and mask.
- **SAW** (symbolic, both legs): each leg returns exactly the Cryptol model's
  packed state: sentinel init, index assignment, byte-offset assignment,
  executed/unexecuted split, and `sepOk`.

The separation bit is the interesting part.  At any executed separator the
byte offset after the opcode is `(bytes consumed so far) + 1 >= index + 1`,
because every parsed opcode consumes at least one byte; later opcodes move
`opcodeIndex` but not `codeSepPos`, so the inequality is preserved.  With
no separator the pair is `(0, 0xFFFFFFFF)`.  Hence the row's two quantities
are never conflated in this domain — a mutant that assigns the index to
`codeSep` is detected by both lanes (`mutant/result.txt`).

The concrete instance of the Lean witness (`pushTwoBytes; CODESEPARATOR`:
`op.next = 4`, index `1`) is in-domain as `02 aa bb ab .. ..`; the cell's
observable reports `codeSep = 4`, `codeSepPos = 1`, `sepOk = 1` there.

## Why the row is still partial

The six-byte bound means at most six parsed opcodes, and the cell does not
execute Core's loop: the `fExec` mask is an input rather than a `vfExec`
derivation, and the pre-checks preceding the separator arm are premises.
Scripts of seven or more bytes (longer pushes, more opcodes) are outside
the bound.  The row's own claim — init, update, and byte-offset separation —
is covered for the whole bounded domain, and every field of the postcondition
is checked by both tools; the residual is the standard bounded-cell seam,
not a missing case of the modelled rule.

Smallest next experiment: raise the bound to eight or ten bytes and add the
`vfExec` stack as an explicit symbolic `IF/ELSE/ENDIF` walk, so the mask is
derived rather than assumed.  That removes the last structural abstraction
but still not the whole-loop claim.
